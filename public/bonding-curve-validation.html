3. Bonding Curve Pricing Model

The curve uses a linear price progression based on the amount of HDFI already sold through the curve. HDFI uses 8 decimal places, and all contract amount values are represented in base units.

1 HDFI = 100,000,000 base units BASE_PRICE = 1,250,000 tinybar per whole HDFI = 0.0125 HBAR per HDFI PRICE_SLOPE = 100 tinybar per additional whole HDFI startPrice = basePrice + (priceSlope × totalMinted / 10^8) endPrice = basePrice + (priceSlope × (totalMinted + amount) / 10^8) averagePrice = (startPrice + endPrice) / 2 totalCost = (averagePrice × amount) / 10^8 Maximum curve allocation = 100,000,000 HDFI

Because the curve is buy-only, totalMinted only increases. As more HDFI is acquired through the curve, the calculated price progresses upward according to the configured slope.

Pricing administration: The contract owner can update the base price and slope through the setPriceParams administrative function. The deployed contract therefore uses owner-controlled curve parameters rather than an immutable pricing schedule.

5. Contract Controls

  • Curve allocation limit: purchases cannot exceed the configured 100,000,000 HDFI curve allocation.
  • Payment validation: the contract requires the supplied HBAR to meet or exceed the calculated purchase cost.
  • HTS transfer validation: HDFI transfers require HTS response code 22.
  • Reentrancy protection: purchase and administrative withdrawal paths use OpenZeppelin's ReentrancyGuard where applicable.
  • Administrative access: price configuration and token/HBAR withdrawals are restricted to the contract owner.
  • Buy-only design: there is no curve-level HDFI redemption or sell function.
  • HTS association: the contract provides an owner-only association function so it can hold the HDFI inventory required for purchases.

6. Treasury and Secondary Liquidity Design

Primary Market

Bonding Curve

The bonding curve is the primary HBAR → HDFI acquisition mechanism. Buyers purchase HDFI directly from the curve contract's pre-funded HTS inventory at a dynamically calculated price.

Secondary Market

Future DEX Liquidity

The bonding curve does not itself provide secondary-market liquidity. The contract includes administrative withdrawal functions so HDFI and accumulated HBAR can be moved to treasury or future liquidity infrastructure, including a SaucerSwap pool.

Important distinction: HBAR held by the bonding curve contract is not represented in this report as DEX liquidity. No claim of an active SaucerSwap liquidity pool is made by this validation report.

7. Existing HedraFi Utility
